a. Define tokenization in NLP. Give an example.
Answer:
Tokenization in NLP is the process of breaking down a text into smaller units called tokens, which could be words, phrases or symbols.
It helps in simplifying text for further analysis and modeling.

Example:
Sentence: "Natural Language Processing is interesting."
After tokenization → ["Natural", "Language", "Processing", "is", "interesting"]


b. What is a Bag of Words model? How does count vectorization differ from one-hot encoding?
Answer:
The Bag of Words (BoW) model is a text representation technique where a document is represented by the frequency of words it contains, without considering grammar or word order.

Difference:
Count Vectorization: Creates a vector with counts of words in a document. For example, if the word "NLP" appears 3 times, its value will be 3.
One-Hot Encoding: Represents each word as a binary vector (0 or 1) for presence or absence. For example, if "NLP" is present, its value is 1, otherwise 0.





c. Explain the purpose of TF-IDF. When is it preferred over simple frequency-based representations?
TF-IDF (Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency) is a weighting scheme that highlights words that are important in a document but not common across all documents.
TF (Term Frequency): How often a word appears in a document.
IDF (Inverse Document Frequency): Penalizes words that appear frequently across many documents (like “the”, “is”).

When preferred?
TF-IDF is preferred over raw counts when we want to reduce the impact of common words and emphasize unique, informative words for tasks like document classification and search engines.




d. What are stop words, and why are they removed in text pre-processing? 
Answer:
Stop words are common words like “is”, “the”, “and”, “in” that carry little or no semantic meaning. They are removed to reduce noise and computational complexity, allowing models to focus on more meaningful words.



e. List any four challenges commonly encountered during text pre-processing.
Answer:
Handling noisy data: Presence of spelling errors, slang, or abbreviations.
Dealing with emojis and symbols: Hard to interpret their meaning in sentiment or semantic analysis.
Normalization issues: Different forms of the same word (e.g., run, running, ran).
Handling multilingual text: Requires language-specific tokenizers and stop word lists.



f. Explain the process and benefits of using Word2Vec embeddings over traditional vectorization techniques.
Answer:
Word2Vec is a neural network-based method that converts words into dense vectors (embeddings) capturing semantic meaning and context.
It uses two main architectures: CBOW (Continuous Bag of Words) and Skip-Gram.
Benefits over BoW or TF-IDF:
Captures semantic similarity (e.g., “king” and “queen” are closer in vector space).
Produces dense, low-dimensional vectors, unlike sparse high-dimensional BoW.
Considers context, making it suitable for tasks like sentiment analysis, machine translation, and recommendation systems.



g. How does semantic analysis differ from sentiment analysis? Illustrate with a use case.
Answer:
Semantic Analysis: Focuses on understanding the meaning and relationships in text. Example: Extracting subject–predicate–object triples from sentences.
Sentiment Analysis: Focuses on determining the emotional tone (positive, negative, neutral).

Use Case:
Semantic Analysis: In a legal document, identifying “Person A purchased Product B”.
Sentiment Analysis: Analyzing product reviews to determine if customers are happy or dissatisfied.


h. Explain the concept of n-gram frequency analysis using unigrams and bigrams. Provide an example from a sample sentence.
Answer:
N-gram analysis involves grouping sequences of n words from text.
Unigrams: Single words
Bigrams: Two consecutive words

Example:
Sentence: "I love natural language processing"
Unigrams: [I, love, natural, language, processing]
Bigrams: [I love, love natural, natural language, language processing]

This analysis is useful for capturing word order and local context in NLP tasks like text prediction.

c. What is the purpose of AFINN lexicon in sentiment analysis?
Answer:
The AFINN lexicon is a list of words associated with integer scores ranging from -5 (negative) to +5 (positive).
It is used to determine the sentiment of text by summing up scores of individual words.
This makes it useful for rule-based or hybrid sentiment analysis approaches.




d. Explain the concept of polarity strength at the sentence level with an example.
Answer:
Polarity strength measures the intensity of sentiment in a sentence, typically on a scale from -1 to +1 or using categorical labels like Strong Positive, Weak Positive, etc.

Example:
Sentence 1: “The movie was good.” → Polarity: +0.4 (Weak Positive)
Sentence 2: “The movie was absolutely fantastic!” → Polarity: +0.9 (Strong Positive)

a. What is the difference between document clustering and topic modeling?
Answer:
Document Clustering: Groups documents into clusters based on similarity without predefined topics.
Each document belongs to one cluster.
Example: News articles grouped into “Sports,” “Politics,” and “Technology.”

Topic Modeling: Identifies latent topics within documents, allowing each document to have multiple topics with associated probabilities.
Example: A news article may contain both “Sports” and “Finance” topics.



b. Define Latent Semantic Analysis (LSA) and explain its role in topic modeling.
Answer:
Latent Semantic Analysis (LSA) is a technique that reduces the dimensionality of term-document matrices using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD).
It captures underlying semantic structures by mapping words and documents to a lower-dimensional latent space.

Role in topic modeling:
Identifies hidden relationships between terms and documents.
Groups words and documents based on co-occurrence patterns, revealing latent topics.

a. What are Large Language Models (LLMs)? How are they different from traditional NLP models?
Answer:
Large Language Models (LLMs) are deep learning models trained on massive datasets to understand and generate human language.
They are based on transformer architectures, which enable context-aware text generation and reasoning.

Difference from traditional NLP models:
Scale: LLMs have billions of parameters, while traditional models (like n-grams or logistic regression) are much smaller.
Learning: LLMs learn from raw text using self-supervised learning, whereas older models rely on hand-crafted features.
Generalization: LLMs handle multiple NLP tasks without task-specific training, unlike traditional models that need separate models for each task.

c. Define prompt engineering and explain why it is important for LLMs.
Answer:
Prompt Engineering is the practice of designing and optimizing input prompts to guide LLMs to produce desired outputs.

Importance:
LLM responses are prompt-dependent, so well-structured prompts improve accuracy.
Reduces hallucinations and improves factual correctness.
Enables zero-shot and few-shot learning without additional training.

Example:
Bad prompt → “Explain AI.”
Better prompt → “Explain Artificial Intelligence in simple terms with two examples.”



d. Explain the architecture and training objectives behind GPT-style decoder-only LLMs
Answer:
GPT models use a decoder-only transformer architecture that processes text from left to right (autoregressive).

Key Components:
Self-Attention Layers: Capture context from previous tokens.
Feed-Forward Networks: Transform token representations.
Layer Normalization & Residual Connections: Stabilize training.

Training Objective:
Predict the next token in a sequence (causal language modeling).
Minimize cross-entropy loss between predicted and actual tokens.

f. Describe the key components of a RAG system and how it improves over zero-shot generation.
Answer:
RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) combines retrieval and generation to enhance accuracy and factuality.

Key Components:
Retriever: Fetches relevant documents from a knowledge base.
Generator: LLM that conditions its response on retrieved documents.
Knowledge Store: A database of domain-specific or external knowledge.

Improvement over Zero-Shot:
Provides grounded answers using external facts.
Reduces hallucination and increases trustworthiness.
Suitable for enterprise apps needing real-time knowledge updates.
